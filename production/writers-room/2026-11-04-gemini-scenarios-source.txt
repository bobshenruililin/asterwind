### Three Scenarios for *Asterwind*

To make a leadership professor lean forward and university students wince in recognition, *Asterwind* shouldn’t treat a group project like a corporate case study. It should treat it like a pressure cooker of ego, exhaustion, and fear. 

In all three pitches below, **"Asterwind"** is the literal artifact the student team is building—and the paper-theatre medium is used as an emotional mirror where the characters and their work are made of the exact same fragile material.

---

#### Pitch 1: *The 3:00 AM Seam* (Excellence vs. Ownership)
* **The Setup:** Architecture students are hours away from their final 8:00 AM atrium critique. They are assembling *Asterwind*—a suspended kinetic canopy of interlocking paper star-vanes designed to spin in the building’s air currents. 
* **The Conflict:** Julian, the earnest, anxious youngest member of the team, begged to build the central rotor hub. He has finally fallen asleep across two studio stools. When you (the team lead) hold his finished hub up to the desk lamp, the backlit vellum reveals the truth: his scoring is crooked and the glue is globbed. Under the gallery fans tomorrow, it will wobble and jam. You have two hours, a fresh surgical blade, and spare cardstock.
* **The Choice:** Do you quietly recut and replace Julian’s piece while he sleeps so the team gets an A+, or do you wake him to splint and reinforce his crooked hub together—knowing the final sculpture will visibly limp in front of the faculty?
* **The Leadership Nuance:** The trap of the "Overfunctioning Leader." Saving a project in secret guarantees short-term excellence at the cost of castrating your teammate’s agency.

#### Pitch 2: *Ballast* (The Rubric vs. The Soul)
* **The Setup:** An interdisciplinary team of engineering and art students is on a wind-swept roof at dawn, preparing to launch *Asterwind*—a high-altitude paper-and-balsa weather kite. 
* **The Conflict:** The morning wind is weaker than forecasted. The rig is 150 grams too heavy to clear the parapet. To make it fly before the professor’s timer runs out, you must unclip one of two components: Maya’s barometric sensor array (which accounts for 40% of the syllabus rubric) or Dev’s paper wind-organ (an ungraded acoustic chamber that whistles chords in flight—the passion piece that kept the team from quitting during midterms).
* **The Choice:** Cut the wind-organ (the kite flies silently and scores high, breaking the team's spirit) or cut the sensor array (the kite sings across the sky, failing the technical rubric).
* **The Leadership Nuance:** Transactional compliance vs. protective vision. What happens to a team when a leader optimizes purely for external metrics versus protecting what gave the work meaning?

#### Pitch 3: *The Ghost in the Credits* (Public Unity vs. Moral Courage)
* **The Setup:** Animation students are backstage behind a backlit shadow-screen, presenting their short film *Asterwind* to a packed auditorium and a visiting studio recruiter.
* **The Conflict:** Seconds before the Q&A begins, you discover that Soren—your brilliant, ruthless lead animator—secretly swapped out the messy, hand-cut storm sequence made by Lin (who was caring for a sick parent all week) with his own sleek, laser-cut version. The film just ended to thunderous applause. The recruiter steps up to the mic and asks: *"That storm sequence was breathtaking—who designed that movement?"*
* **The Choice:** Pass the microphone to Soren to preserve the electric triumph in the room, or take the microphone yourself and expose the cut in front of the audience.
* **The Leadership Nuance:** Complicity through silence. How easily leaders trade away justice for the intoxicating high of collective prestige.

### Full Scene Development: *The 3:00 AM Seam*

**Runtime:** ~3.5 minutes  
**Visual Style:** Multi-plane digital paper theatre. Everything is constructed from tactile paper textures: warm Manila cardstock, translucent tracing vellum, corrugated cardboard shadows, and brass paper-fasteners for character joints.  
**Audio Style:** Intimate foley—the *scritch* of graphite, the crisp *snap* of heavy paper, the hum of a fluorescent tube, a radiator clanking against the winter wind outside.

---

#### ACT I: THE STUDIO (0:00 – 1:30)

**VISUAL:**  
The proscenium opens by unfolding like an architectural blueprint. Three depth planes compose the 4th-floor studio at 3:42 AM. 
* **Background:** Tall paper-cut windows washed in freezing indigo light. Snow/wind drifts past outside.
* **Midground:** Julian (constructed from soft, slightly crumpled watercolor paper) is slumped asleep across three stools pushed together, his hoodie pulled tight. 
* **Foreground:** A wide cutting mat bathed in the warm amber cone of a **articulated gooseneck desk lamp**. Above the table hangs *Asterwind*—a breathtaking chandelier of folded white origami stars, nearly complete, except for a gaping hole in its center.

You are **REN** (represented by two expressive, sharp-creased Bristol-board hands at the bottom of the frame).

**ACTION:**  
A sticky note stuck to the lamp shade flutters. The player clicks/taps to peel it off.  
**TEXT ON NOTE (handwritten in sharpie):** *Ren—I’m dead on my feet. Left the central Aster hub on the mat. I swear I measured twice this time. Wake me when we carry it down. —J.*

Ren’s paper hands reach out and lift Julian’s centerpiece—a complex, twelve-pointed paper gear—off the mat, raising it directly in front of the warm bulb of the gooseneck lamp.

**VISUAL STORYTELLING (THE REVEAL):**  
As the vellum piece passes in front of the light source, the theatre uses **real-time backlighting**. To the naked eye, the piece looked fine. Backlit by the lamp, its skeleton is a mess: internal glue blotches look like dark bruises; three load-bearing folds are visibly off-axis by ten degrees; a tear near the center spindle has been clumsily patched with masking tape.

Ren’s hands clip the hub into the hanging *Asterwind* canopy and give it a gentle test spin.  
**AUDIO:** *Whirrrr—whirrrr—CLACK-crunch.*  
The canopy shudders, tilts crookedly, and jams. In the midground, Julian shifts in his sleep, murmuring:  
**JULIAN (VO / Subtitle):** *"Did it balance?"*

---

#### ACT II: THE INTERACTIVE MOMENT (1:30 – 2:15)

**VISUAL:**  
Ren’s hands lower the crooked hub back to the table. On the left side of the cutting mat lies a pristine sheet of uncut 300gsm vellum and a gleaming **brass X-Acto scalpel**. On the right side, just beyond the pool of light, is **Julian’s sleeping silhouette**.

A clock on the back wall ticks to **4:00 AM**. Only one hour remains before the glue must cure for the 8:00 AM critique.

**THE MECHANIC (NO UI, PURE DIEGESIS):**  
There are no dialogue boxes or A/B buttons. Instead, the cursor highlights the **brass handle of the Gooseneck Desk Lamp** at the top of the frame. The player grabs the lamp head and drags it.

* **As you drag the light Left (over the fresh vellum and scalpel):** The blade glints. A clean geometric stencil projection appears on the fresh paper. Meanwhile, Julian’s sleeping figure on the right is swallowed completely by black shadow. He ceases to exist in the frame.
* **As you drag the light Right (toward Julian):** The cutting mat and scalpel fall into darkness. The warm amber light spills across Julian’s slumped shoulders and illuminates a roll of heavy gold drafting tape sitting between you and him.

The player can pan the light back and forth for a moment, watching who and what the light erases, before releasing the lamp to lock in their choice.

---

#### ACT III-A: IF YOU LIGHT THE SCALPEL (The Martyr’s Masterpiece)

**VISUAL & AUDIO (2:15 – 3:00):**  
Darkness swallows Julian. Only the tight amber circle on the cutting mat remains.  
Ren’s hands move with ruthless, hypnotic speed.  
*Snick. Snick. Snick.*  
Paper shavings curl into the air like snow. Through a rapid shadow-puppet montage, Ren scores, folds, and bonds a brand-new central hub with razor-sharp precision. Ren’s own paper hands visibly fray and crease at the knuckles from the strain.

With one harsh motion, Ren’s hand sweeps Julian’s crooked hub off the edge of the table. We hear it land softly in the wastebasket below.

**THE CLIMAX (3:00 – 3:45):**  
The dark studio flats slide apart, revealing the blinding, sunlit white paper expanse of the **University Atrium**.  
Silhouette cutouts of students and the **Professor** (tall, angular, dark charcoal cardstock) gather below. Above them hangs *Asterwind*.

The atrium vents kick on.  
**AUDIO:** A rushing, melodic wind chime sound.  
*Asterwind* catches the draft and begins to spin. It is weightless, symmetrical, and hypnotic—casting interlocking star-shadows across the entire stage.

**PROFESSOR:** *"Flawless tolerances. It moves like a single organism. Easily the strongest rig of the semester."*

**THE COST (THE FINAL IMAGE):**  
The camera pans slowly down from the spinning canopy to the floor.  
Ren stands front-and-center in the bright light.  
Two depth-planes back, standing near a structural column, is Julian. He is staring up at the central hub—and then down at his own hands. He recognizes immediately that those aren't his seams.  

As the audience watches, a subtle, devastating visual transformation occurs on Julian’s paper cutout: the warm watercolor texture drains out of his figure, leaving him flat, blank, uncreased grey cardstock. He folds his hands into his pockets, takes one quiet step backward behind the column flat, and disappears from the scene while the applause continues.

*Fade to black on the sound of the spinning paper canopy.*

---

#### ACT III-B: IF YOU LIGHT JULIAN (The Splinted Wing)

**VISUAL & AUDIO (2:15 – 3:00):**  
The warm lamp swings onto Julian. He blinks awake, shielding his eyes with a crumpled paper hand. He sees the crooked hub sitting in the light between you.

**JULIAN:** *"Oh. No, wait—the glue pulled while it dried. Give me the knife, I can start over—"*  
**REN:** *"It’s 4:00 AM, Jules. There’s no time to recut."*

Julian’s paper figure literally creases inward at the chest, shrinking in size on screen.  
**JULIAN:** *"I ruined the final."*

Ren’s hand enters the frame—not with the scalpel, but pushing the roll of **heavy gold drafting tape** and a handful of **brass balsa-splints** into the center of the light.  
**REN:** *"Then help me splint the torque. Show me where the fold gave out."*

Four hands—two sharp Bristol, two soft watercolor—enter the circle of light together. It isn't pretty work. They bind the crooked joints with visible gold tape and counter-weight the off-axis star points with brass clips.

**THE CLIMAX (3:00 – 3:45):**  
The studio flats slide apart into the blinding white **University Atrium**.  
The Professor and students gather below. The vents kick on.

*Asterwind* catches the wind.  
**AUDIO:** *Whirrrr—shhhk—whirrrr—shhhk.*  
It spins, but with a distinct, rhythmic hitch—like a mechanical heartbeat. Every rotation catches the sunlight on the thick gold tape and heavy brass splints at its core. It tilts at a visible five-degree list, stubbornly refusing to fall, turning through sheer counter-balanced grit.

**PROFESSOR:** *(Tilting his head, notebook raised)* *"The canopy aerodynamics are lovely, Ren... but your central axis is visibly eccentric. Those gold splints are adding drag. Why didn’t you just recut the core?"*

A heavy beat of silence hangs in the theatre. Ren’s figure shifts to speak—

**THE PAYOFF (THE FINAL IMAGE):**  
Before Ren can answer, Julian’s figure slides forward from the midground plane right up to the front edge of the stage, standing shoulder-to-shoulder with Ren directly in the brightest light.

**JULIAN:** *"Because my initial scoring failed under torsion, Professor. We had forty minutes before cure-time, so we chose field-reinforcement over a rush-recut. If you look at the third spline, you can see how we redirected the load..."*

As Julian speaks, pointing upward, the camera pulls back.  
Above them, the scarred, gold-seamed canopy keeps turning—imperfect, audible, and alive. On the floor of the paper stage, cast by the bright gallery lights, Ren and Julian’s shadows stretch across the white paper floor—identical in size.

*Fade to black.*

### Why This Works for Your Professor & Students

1. **No Moralizing Summary Screen:** When the curtain falls, there is no pop-up saying *"Good leaders empower others!"* Instead, the screen simply fades to a single prompt for the classroom or player to sit with:  
   > *In Path A, the project got an A. In Path B, the project got a B+. Which failure were you more willing to live with?*
2. **Medium as Metaphor:** By using paper theatre’s core properties—**backlighting** (to reveal hidden flaws), **shadow casting** (to show status and presence), and **paper texture** (showing burnout and erasure)—the mechanics of the art form carry the emotional weight rather than long blocks of dialogue.
