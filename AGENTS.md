# Asterwind contributor instructions

## Film-first interface and composition

- Let the layout, composition, performance, sound and consequences explain the scene. Avoid unnecessary secondary labels, descriptions, badges and explanatory panels.
- Integrate interaction into the film and its images: the relevant character or object should be the action target. Do not place a separate quiz or dashboard over the film merely to explain an interaction.
- Keep one meaningful, consequential choice per scenario, between two clear actions. Let setup and consequences play naturally; do not require repeated forward-clicking, precision dragging or extra inspection gates.
- Minimal visible text must not make the choice obscure. Use staging, focus, restrained affordances and immediate feedback so players understand what is actionable and what their action does. Add short contextual wording only where it is needed for comprehension.
- Preserve accessible alternatives: semantic controls and meaningful accessible names, keyboard access and visible focus, generous touch targets, optional captions, pause/replay and reduced-motion support. Accessibility information need not become decorative visible labels.
- Keep essential context available through dialogue or another accessible equivalent. Never rely on colour, hover, precise pointer movement or sound alone.
- Treat optional post-choice perspectives as part of the cinematic experience, not a mandatory explanatory dashboard or another consequential choice. Do not reveal emotional outcomes in pre-choice hover previews.
- Review the whole sequence as a story, not only individual assets. Every image, transition and control should support character, tension, meaning or consequence.

Read the relevant project handoff and creative-direction documents before implementation, preserving source attribution and existing authorization boundaries. This file adds design guidance; it does not authorize publication, spending, account changes or overriding user instructions.
