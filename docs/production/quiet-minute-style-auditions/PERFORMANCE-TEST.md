# The next proof: acting, not another attractive still

## The question

Can the chosen medium carry the small change between Nia trying to speak, being interrupted, and insisting on a fact, while the same character, room, route and decision survive?

## Smallest useful test

Make a **single locked-camera performance test**, followed by one choice moment. Keep it optional and outside the default game until reviewed.

1. **Nia begins:** "The courtyard gate closes—" Eyes toward Theo, a small intake of breath, one hand keeps the route open.
2. **Interruption lands:** Theo: "We'll be through by then." Nia's mouth closes, her brows and grip register that the concern has not been heard. No melodramatic collapse.
3. **Nia corrects:** "We arrive after it closes." Same head and body, a more definite eye line and measured opening of the mouth.
4. **Ellis waits:** pencil lowers. A thumb presses an existing fold in a compact route packet. This is restraint, not a generic sad pose.
5. **Decision hold:** Ren's indigo hands pause between model and slips. The two actions read without labels covering the scene.

Use the existing dialogue and timing as the authority. Do not invent new facts, append extra choices or reveal branch outcomes before selection.

## Production method to investigate

- Lock a fresh **approved** character sheet before generating a pose family. This audition is not itself the replacement canon sheet.
- Separate a clean room plate, characters, hand/prop overlaps, model, slips and light where the test needs independent motion.
- Use complete replacement expressions/poses at story beats. Do not mistake rotating a flat face or sliding a whole character for acting.
- Retain exact route geometry, pencil grip, glasses, folds and costume colours across the test.
- A depth map can improve a camera move. It does not supply the missing surface behind an occlusion, a mouth rig or a new expression.
- If a video tool would incur credits, stop for Bob's approval before generation. This package authorizes no provider spend.

## Review gate

Compare the two methods on the **same** brief rather than comparing their best unrelated shots.

- Is Nia the same recognisable adult in each beat?
- Does the interruption read without a caption explaining the emotion?
- Do glasses, fingers, hair, costume weave and route markings stay stable?
- Does Theo remain competent but too quick, rather than becoming a villain?
- Does Ellis's lowered pencil still have narrative meaning?
- Can both action objects be understood at 390 px wide?
- Are the two choices usable by keyboard and touch?
- Does reduced motion preserve the same story and choice?
- Does the branch consequence read as a trade-off rather than a reward screen?

Mark actual failures, not just an average beauty score. If one method cannot hold identity or hands, simplify the shot or reject that method before any asset-wide regeneration.

## What must wait

Whole-game restyling, replacement of the film plates, canonical-cast changes and any runtime default switch wait for Bob's choice and a successful performance test. No runtime implementation is included here.
