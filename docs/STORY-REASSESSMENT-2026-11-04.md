## The 3:00 AM Seam (`backup`, `repair`)

**Wants.** Mara: working footage by eight for a fellowship that closes at noon, and the spare she cut at midnight used. Julian: to be there when the rib he designed moves. Ren (the player): a call that gets the model set by 4:30 without someone vanishing from the account of the night.

**What the player chooses.** Fit Mara's spare, or spend the remaining time splinting Julian's original together.

**What happens now.** Backup: "Julian, do you want a break?" → he steps away → the test runs without him → "I missed it" → "We kept going. I should have checked with you." Repair: the canopy holds by half; Mara's footage is weaker; "I made that call."

**Diagnosis.** In the backup branch the felt cost (Julian left out) is produced by Ren not checking with him, a communication lapse the player never chose. A player who picks the backup *and* would include Julian has no way to. The backup is being punished for something else, and the two branches stop being comparable. This is exactly Dot's note.

**Proposal.** Keep both actions and outcomes. Keep everyone present in both branches, so each branch's cost is intrinsic to the choice:
- **Backup:** Ren includes Julian in fitting the spare (for example "Use the spare. Julian, show us where it has to sit — it's your geometry"). The canopy opens on time and Mara has her footage. The cost is Julian's *design*, not his presence: what moves is not the seam he drew, and his original stays cracked beside it. A closing line in his own voice registers that honestly, without blame (for example "It moves. Not the way I drew it.").
- **Repair:** unchanged in substance. Julian's seam moves, half of it holds, and the cost lands on Mara's footage.
- **Common close:** keep the shared account. Both people record what happened, including the branch-specific model.

Each choice now trades one person's stake against the other's, with no hidden failure of inclusion. Why either: the backup protects a real external deadline with work that already fits; the repair keeps the original design alive and gives its author authorship of the result.

**Changes if approved.** Backup branch lines 1–2 and the "I should have checked with you" beat; one new performance plate (Julian awake and guiding the spare's fit, hands in shot), which replaces the treatment's "sleeping Julian" work insert. Timing tested afterwards. Completion rules unchanged.

**Optional, for the difficult-feedback theme (see below):** one setup line where Ren tells Julian plainly and kindly that the rib has failed three times and won't hold by 4:30 as it is. That is specific feedback in the setup, not a second choice.

## The Missing Name (`microphone`, `notebook`)

**Wants.** Asha: her fieldwork recognised as hers; she consents to either opening and can explain the fieldwork but not the whole pitch. Kit: the planned arc that ends on the model. You: correct the attribution before starting.

**What the player chooses.** Give Asha the microphone to open with her own framing, or credit her yourself with her notebook visible and keep the planned pitch.

**What happens now.** Microphone: Asha frames the work and the audience leans in; "the slot ends before the **decorative** model reveal." Notebook: you credit her by name, Kit's planned pitch runs with the model, and Asha answers a visitor's question when Kit invites her.

**Diagnosis.** Consent and credit hold in both routes, as they should. But the microphone route's only cost is labelled decorative, so the competing use of presentation time doesn't matter and the choice tilts.

**Proposal.** Make the time that competes with Asha's opening carry real weight for someone else, without making anyone a villain. The final model becomes **Kit's working demonstration**: the part that shows the route working with the people who use it, and the part Kit built. Keep Asha's consent and named credit in both routes.
- **Microphone:** Asha opens in her own words. The slot ends before Kit's model runs, so the judges see the map's origin but not the model working, and Kit's work stays in its box. The cost lands on Kit's recognition and the evidence, which mirrors the film's theme from the other side.
- **Notebook:** you credit Asha by name with her notebook visible. Kit's demonstration runs, and Asha answers the visitor's question in her own words when invited. The cost is that her work is framed by someone else, and she speaks only when asked.

Why either: one protects the author's own voice; the other protects a teammate's work and the presentation's evidence while still naming the author.

**Changes if approved.** Mostly text: Kit's trade-off line names what is at stake ("…we lose the model demonstration — the part I built"), plus the microphone ending caption. Existing closed-box and open-model plates may suffice; check them before requesting art.

## Comparison with Hannah and Amanda's workbook (as held in `app/scenarios.js`)

Workbook situations: Q1 unreachable teammate, Q2 late dancer, Q3 ten-minute emergency, Q4 after a disappointing event, Q5 weak slides, Q6 day one, Q7 influence without a title, Q8 the loudest voice, Q9 friend versus better candidate.

The Seam comes closest, but it jumps from the failure straight to a resource call. *Proposal:* the optional Seam setup line above, delivered as a performance with no extra choice.    - **One More Chair:** make Inez the coordinator and You a volunteer. The extend action becomes a time-boxed offer you own ("Give me twenty minutes on the bag; I'll stop at 5:40 and finish the umbrella myself"), Inez's own call follows, and the cost lands on you. This is Q7's small-trial pattern, inside the existing choice.
   - **Before the Doors Open:** make Dev the exhibit lead. You propose the live run as one disclosed trial at the side table, so its result has to earn the change.

Neither turns a workbook "best answer" into a correct branch. Both actions stay defensible, the films show consequences rather than scores, and workbook feedback stays in the separate nine-scenario `app/`.
