## 1. Direction Bob stated in the 3 November conversation

Bob wants a multi-model creative workflow: send prompts to creative non-GPT models on Poe, bring the outputs back, and develop the work through human editorial judgment. Potential interactions he explicitly wants explored include selecting a character as the person to trust; revisiting what could have happened otherwise; and clicking a character's head/brain region to reveal thoughts or perceptions arising from another person's action or speech. These examples are development possibilities, not approval to implement all of them in every episode.

## 2. Editorial recommendation — not yet a locked design

Working premise: **you choose an action, but you do not fully control what it means to another person.** This is a proposed series theme, not a replacement for the source scenarios.

Make the short film worth watching without clicking. Then make its single consequential gesture add something a passive film cannot: responsibility, attention, trust, perspective, or a felt trade-off. Do not add interaction only to show a technical capability.

Use one signature interaction per episode. Optional exploration and replay are not extra consequential decisions. Looking into a fictional character's interior should change the viewer's understanding, not award points or expose a guaranteed best answer.

An interior-head sequence can use a paper anatomical cutaway as a visual entrance, then unfold into a small stage of memory, fear and unspoken response. Treat it as fictional interiority, not anatomical instruction, psychological measurement, or inferred knowledge of real classmates. Keep one focal thought or image at a time. Do not claim literal brain regions contain particular sentences.

Counterfactual replay should identify the unchosen path and retain the original choice unless the player explicitly restarts. It is one authored possible alternative, not a scientific prediction. Avoid a completionist catalogue of endings that dissolves the emotional stakes.

## 3. Audience and goals — working proposal

Assume university peers and the course audience are primary until the actual assessment requirements are checked. A portfolio/director's view can be secondary.

Audience-facing film: an immediate dramatic opening, clear action affordances, sparse dialogue/subtitles, no dashboard, moral score or opening slide of learning objectives.

Reflection or presenter view: explain the selected source dilemma, the intended learning question, what the audience noticed, and the relevant course connection after the dramatic experience. Do not claim an educational benefit has been validated by the prototype. Check explicit rubric requirements before omitting required goal statements.

The work should remain enjoyable for a viewer who does not care about leadership training. Humour, specificity and unresolved feeling are welcome; compulsory solemnity and concluding lectures are not.

## 6. Common Poe audition prompt

Act as a screenwriter and interactive-film director, not a consultant. Create original material for Asterwind: a tactile paper-theatre short-film series in a floating academy, with fictional adult students and a university peer audience. Make the film engaging even for someone uninterested in leadership training.

Invent a 60–90-second episode about the gap between intention, action and another person's interpretation. If no source scenario is supplied, label it ORIGINAL CONCEPT — NOT SOURCE-MAPPED.

There is exactly one consequential decision with two legible actions. Embed it in a person or object in the scene, not a disconnected menu. Its meaning must be clear before commitment. Optional perspective exploration and counterfactual replay must not introduce more consequential choices or timed automatic selection.

Give both principal characters a concrete want and a defensible reason. Do not create a saint and a fool, imply all actions are equally harmless, or reveal that one character was secretly perfect. Keep dialogue sparse and speakable. Begin with action or a compelling image, not explanatory narration. Include one surprising visual metaphor that makes the relationship clearer. Show consequences without a score or concluding lecture.

Limit the proposal to three on-screen characters, one main set, one transformed inner-world set and six principal shots. Use sound, silence and editing, not endless effects. A head/interior reveal depicts a fictional character's authored thoughts; it is not real-world mind reading or neuroscience.

Deliver: three sharply different premises of at most 70 words each, the third your risky proposal; select one and write a timed screenplay with sound, sparse dialogue and the exact decision frame; write the two endings separately, with a real difference and plausible costs; describe one optional perspective reveal and a brief counterfactual; list reusable assets and the hardest production dependency; identify the weakest beat and rewrite it more boldly. Stay under 1,200 words. No opening praise, code, sales pitch or claims of a completed film.

## 7. Proposed production gates

Audience/source lock -> independent writing audition -> chosen screenplay -> rough timed stills and temporary voices -> approved reference assets -> short motion tests -> interactive edit -> viewer review.

Do not spend polished-video effort before the rough film makes emotional sense. The asset package should cover character identity, expressions/poses, performance/voice direction, set, key props, sound motifs and deliberately composed interaction frames, not only beautiful images.

At the choice, settle into a stable frame with generous selectable targets. Use short hover/focus labels plus keyboard-equivalent controls. Do not depend on precise clicking on moving heads. Prepare the dramatic branches in advance rather than generating unreviewed dialogue live during the classroom demonstration.

A few viewer tests should examine what each person wanted, what the player believed they were choosing, whose response surprised them, and what they remember. These are design observations, not a validated learning-outcome study.
