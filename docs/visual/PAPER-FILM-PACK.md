# What the shadows say: reference pack and frame list

5 November 2026, from Opus to Robert, for the [paper-film proof](../demos/paper-film/index.html). Status: **proposal**. The proof is cut in code from card shapes so we can judge the idea now. Everything in it is meant to be replaced by your frames, one for one.

## The idea

Paper is our superpower, not a cheaper photograph. The campus is grounded. Once we drink, the paper world can bend reality to show what someone feels, what a choice costs, and what nobody says.

In this scene the bending lives in the **shadows**. A low lamp on the table throws everyone's shadow huge on the wall, and the shadows tell the truth:
- **Mara.** She looks up at Ren, but her shadow keeps watching her phone. Its phone light grows into a cold screen on the warm wall, framing the design: the footage is her future.
- **Julian.** His shadow cradles the model's shadow, and its louvres beat like a wing. To him it's alive.
- **The player** picks something up and does it with their own paper hands: slot the spare, or wrap the crack three times.
- **After the spare:** Julian takes his break, but **his shadow stays behind on the wall** with his folded design, still there in daylight when the canopy opens perfectly.
- **After the splint:** all their shadows hold one thing. At dawn the canopy opens only halfway, and her screen comes back with two bars missing.

Every line is approved. Nothing in the story's causality changes.

## Two ways to make it (your call)

1. **Cut-out kits, posed by code.** You paint each part once, in character, and the code poses, steps and casts them. This gives the most consistency, the most interactivity and the fewest images. Shadows come free: they're the same parts in silhouette with their own poses.
2. **Full keyframes, stepped by code.** You paint each key pose as a complete registered frame, for richer light and expression. The code steps between them at 12 fps and lays the shadow theatre and the controls over them.

You could also mix them: painted keyframes for the set's light states, and cut-out kits for the figures and hands.

## Consistency rules

- **Camera:** one locked camera, with the table line and figure scale fixed. The lamp sits low at front-centre; the window is high on the left.
- **Light:** at night every shadow is cast by the lamp and at dawn by the window, **except the deliberate truths**: a shadow posed differently from its owner, the screen of light, and the shadow that stays behind.
- **Identity:** Mara's pencil bun and phone, Julian's smudge and hair, and the six louvres, in every frame.
- **Words on screen:** no generated text anywhere. Captions and the few controls are code.
