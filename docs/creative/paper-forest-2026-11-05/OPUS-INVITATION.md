## A possible first film experiment

M01, the intimate Listening Tide, is a promising low-complexity starting point. It already gives two people, one light and a small sheltered fold. Consider three beats:

1. Stay with the speaker long enough to feel the uneven rhythm of her hands and the paper tide.
2. Let the listener place the lantern carefully. The gesture makes a little room; it does not drain the sea.
3. Wait for the speaker's own response. She may move the boat, protect the fold, continue talking or leave the silence intact.

This is a suggestion to improve, not a selected scene or an instruction to generate paid video. A rough sequence of held frames, marked camera moves, sound intentions and one carefully isolated motion could answer more than a polished full transformation. If another image would teach us more, make that case.

For a bolder direction, compare W06's public amphitheater with C01's divided waterways. One makes a quiet idea visible at city scale; the other makes a small decision costly at city scale. The useful question is not which image looks grander, but which relationship can sustain a sequence.

## Things to protect while exploring

- Preserve agency. A person chooses to unfold a private room, accept help or share a burden. Another character does not force the revelation.
- Keep meaningful consequences. A beautiful transformation should leave a change in work, trust, resources or responsibility.
- Give both possible actions a reason. If one is obviously cruel and the other obviously kind, the film has stopped asking a human question.
- Let the frame do the work. Gesture, staging, sound and a change in the material can carry meaning before we add text.
- Keep quiet scenes in the forest. Not every feeling needs a city to rearrange itself.

The 21 stills explore a visual vocabulary. They do not establish fixed characters, plot, sequencing, licensed music, motion production approval or new mechanics. Keep existing story and runtime work safe on its own branch. If we move toward an interactive proof, retain accessible equivalents, keyboard and touch access, captions where needed, pause/replay and reduced motion from the project guidance.
