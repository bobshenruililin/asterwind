# Kling: where generated motion earns its place

6 November 2026, Opus. **Nothing has been generated or spent, and no access is assumed.** Bob decides access and credits.

## The rule

Authored interaction carries everything the player does. The living stage already gives stills depth, breath and small acting. Kling is only for one physical or performed action that code can't make believable, and:
- it never happens at a choice;
- it plays only after a commit or inside an optional look;
- it's always skippable, with a still fallback;
- it's muted (our sound is designed separately);
- it's judged inside the played scene, not on its own.

## The limited experiment

1. Robert delivers P2a and P2b, the two still frames of Ellis's hands, closed and open, with the same framing.
2. Generate K1 three times at 720p, 5 s, silent, from P2a, ending as close to P2b as the model allows.
3. **Accept only if** all of these hold:
   - the hands and sleeves keep their identity;
   - the paper opens along real folds, with no new creases or morphing;
   - the route marks match P2b;
   - it reads at 1280×720 inside the scene.
4. Integrate it behind the optional look, then compare it in the scene against the warp-and-dissolve fallback.
   - **Keep it only if it's clearly better.**
   - **If it isn't, stop.** Generated motion doesn't serve this world, and we don't spend more.
