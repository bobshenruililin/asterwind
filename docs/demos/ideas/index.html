Step back

What if leading were the controls?

The game today asks one question six times: here are two objects, which one? It teaches that every choice keeps something and costs something, and that’s worth keeping. But the more we added around that one question (corridor, bottles, intros, labels, reflections, notebook), the more it became a labyrinth around a single verb.

Real leading is made of smaller verbs, and each one can be a control a player feels in their hands. These are ten attempts, each built tonight as a short playable sketch with our own art, voices and stories. Each makes one verb the whole interaction, so you learn it by doing it, not by being told.

If I folded three of these into the game

One verb per story. Keep the six stories and the night, but give each story its own control instead of the same two-object choice:

  • listening in The Quiet Minute;
  • asking in One More Chair;
  • holding the room in The Missing Name;
  • saying it in The 3:00 AM Seam.

The decision still lands, but each story teaches a different skill, and the night stops feeling like the same puzzle six times.

End on notes, not a summary. At dawn, the people you led leave notes (idea 10), next to your rule card if you wrote one at the start (idea 7).

A seminar mode. “Two players, one call” (idea 8) for CCGL classes. It needs no new art.

Not built tonight

  • The ripple. One cast across the night, so your choices come back. Sam turns up at the exhibit; Mara remembers the 3 am call when Asha needs a slot.
  • The empty chair. Every scene has a seat for the person who isn’t there (Mrs Okafor, the queue outside), and you can sit in it.
  • Rewind, remembered. You can go back and choose again, but the characters remember that you did.
  • Say it aloud. Speak your sentence to Julian with the microphone instead of picking strips. It’s powerful, but accuracy and privacy need care first.
Every sketch runs offline from the repository’s own files: no frameworks, no network. The art and voices are the game’s. The Empty Square is left out of these sketches until its lived-experience review.
