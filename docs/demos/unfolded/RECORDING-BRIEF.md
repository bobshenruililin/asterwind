# Recording brief: HEARD, in our own voices

6 November 2026, Opus. Two voices, ten lines, about 30 minutes with retakes. The game already has a slot for them.

## How it plugs in

1. Name each file by its cue id (`heard-01.wav` …) and drop it in `voices/team/`.
2. List it under `sets.team.cues` in `voices/index.json`, or send the files to me and I'll trim, level and list them.
3. In the game, switch on sound (♪). The **Voices** button then cycles through *off · team · scratch*, so you can compare your recording with the scratch voices line by line.

**The scratch set is Kokoro-82M** (Apache-2.0), open-licence text-to-speech. It's there only to time the scene and to compare against. **No paid voice service is used or needed.** If we ever want one, that's Bob's call, after this comparison.

## The people

**Ellis** (a student on the committee in *The Quiet Minute*)
- Quick mind and careful mouth. Ellis drafts in the head, and by the time it's ready the room has moved on.
- Sincere, never self-pitying. "I love it" is true: Ellis does love Theo's route. That's what makes it hard.
- **Warm and dry.** Say it to one person sitting close, at night, by water. No projection.
- **Where it breaks:** Ellis speeds up when getting near the real thing, then stops dead.
- **Casting:** anyone who can sound like they're telling the truth slightly against their will. Gender doesn't matter, and no line uses a pronoun.

**Ren** ("you")
- Steady, plain, not a teacher-voice. Ren under-plays; the line should sound like a decision, not a speech.
- **Casting:** anyone. Ren has no pronoun anywhere, so cast the reading, not the person.

## The script

The cue ids match the game. Record three takes of each, with the third quieter than you think it should be.

| Cue | Who | Line | Direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| heard-01 | Ellis | Theo's route ends here. At the harbour. | Looking at the water, not at Ren. A small pause after "here". |
| heard-02 | Ellis | Everyone will love it. I love it. | Mean both. The second sentence is quieter and almost surprised. |
| heard-03 | Ellis | Mine starts somewhere else. Every time I open it, someone's already— | Speeding up into it. Cut off on "already", as if a wave hit, not trailing away. |
| heard-o1 | Ren | Bring it tomorrow. I'll make sure Theo hears it first. | A promise, not a rescue. Even, low. |
| heard-o2 | Ellis | …You'd say it? | Relief and a sliver of loss in the same breath. |
| heard-o3 | Ellis | Yes. You say it. They listen to you. | "They listen to you" is true, and a little sad. Don't play the sadness. |
| heard-l1 | Ellis | It's not Theo. | After a long silence: let the silence happen in the room before you speak. |
| heard-l2 | Ellis | I only show things when they're finished. And it's never finished. | A half-laugh at yourself on the second sentence, then gone. |
| heard-l3 | Ellis | It doesn't even reach the sea. | Looking at the open paper. Plain fact; that's what hurts. |
| heard-l4 | Ellis | I'll bring it. Myself. | Deciding while saying it. "Myself" is a separate, smaller sentence. |

**Wild lines (optional, very useful):**
- Ellis: a breath in before speaking (×3); a small "mm" (×2); a short, surprised laugh (×2).
- Ren: a breath (×2).
- **Room tone:** 15 seconds of the room with nobody speaking.

## Practical recording

- **Room:** the deadest room you can find. A wardrobe full of clothes, or a small room with a duvet hung behind the speaker. Never a bathroom or a classroom.
- **Phone or microphone:**
  - **Phone:** a phone works. Use a voice-memo app set to its highest quality (WAV or lossless if it offers it), with the phone 20–30 cm from the mouth, slightly to one side so "p" and "b" don't pop. Flight mode on.
  - **USB microphone:** set it to 48 kHz, 24-bit, with peaks around −12 dB and never into the red.
- **Takes:** say the cue id out loud before each take ("heard-03, take 2"), wait a second, then the line. It makes trimming trivial.
- **Performance:**
  - Sit down: the characters are sitting.
  - Speak to a person, not the microphone. Put someone opposite the speaker if you can.
  - Keep it small. Paper puppets need voices that sound like they're in the room, not on stage.
- **Delivery:**
  - Send the raw files and say which take you like. I'll cut, de-noise lightly, and level them to −20 LUFS to match the scratch set.
  - Originals are kept untouched.

## Consent and credit

- Everyone who records agrees, in writing (a reply in PR #1 is enough), that their voice may be used in Asterwind and its demos, and how they want to be credited.
- Anyone can withdraw their voice later; we'll remove it.
- Under-18s need a parent or guardian's agreement.
- Credits and dates go in `voices/team/PROVENANCE.md`.

## What we're comparing

Listen to the whole room at least twice per set, ideally with someone who hasn't seen it:

1. Does Ellis sound like a person, or like someone reading?
2. Does the silence before "It's not Theo." land?
3. Does the cut-off on "already—" feel like the wave interrupted?
4. Is Ren's promise a promise?

My expectation: real voices win on 1 and 2 by a long way, and that difference matters more than any image upgrade.
